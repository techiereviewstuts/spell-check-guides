Grammarly Alternative for Everyday Typing: What to Check Before You Install

By Konstantinos Kreouzis, founder of TypoGuard · 12 November 2026

Many people search for a Grammarly alternative for one simple reason: they do not need a full writing suite. They want typos fixed in emails, chats and forms, without a long setup or a high price. This page gives a short checklist for choosing among the apps like Grammarly for free, and shows where TypoGuard fits.

Decide what you actually need

Writing tools fall into roughly three groups: full suites with style and tone rewriting, websites where you paste text, and browser extensions that check while you type. If your main problem is typos and wrong words in everyday messages, the third group is usually the closest match. If you edit long reports or need tone suggestions, you may want a suite instead, or both.

Five things to check before you install

  • Where it works. Test it in the places you really write: your inbox, chat apps and any forms you use daily.
  • How it fixes. One click inline is faster than copying text to a side panel.
  • Free plan limits. Look for the monthly allowance, and whether it is a words limit or a corrections limit.
  • Language support. If you write in more than one language, check each one, not just English.
  • Privacy. Any tool that reads what you type deserves a careful look, as explained below.

Why privacy deserves a real look

A spell checker extension sees your text. Google's Chrome Web Store User Data FAQ explains that extensions handling personal or sensitive user data must post a privacy policy and handle that data securely. Before installing, open the extension's listing, read the privacy policy, and check that the tool describes what it sends and why. This takes a minute and is worth it for any extension that works inside your email and messages.
